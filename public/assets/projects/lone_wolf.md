# BSc Thesis — Lone Wolf synthetic test environment (Spring 2026)

This bachelor thesis, written with KONGSBERG, made the Lone Wolf autonomy stack easier to develop, verify, and regression test without constant access to the physical platform. The result is a modular, configurable synthetic test environment in NVIDIA Isaac Sim for the planned Lone Wolf UGV, designed to support future platform, payload, and sensor configurations.

The work focused on interface consistency (/clock, TF, topics), repeatable scenario execution, and closed-loop testing, so scenarios can be reproduced and changes validated reliably across project cycles. Assets and payloads are handled modularly, so sensors and configurations can be swapped without breaking the workflow. By shifting more testing into a controlled simulation environment, the project reduced the cost and friction of field trials while improving traceability and confidence in changes.

The delivery is a simulation workflow usable as a practical extension of the physical platform, and a foundation for synthetic data generation and perception testing. Carried out in a team of four, January–June 2026.

The thesis is registered in the Norwegian national research archive: [read the publication record at Sikt NVA](https://nva.sikt.no/registration/019fdbfa5b5b-f2a75d0c-5d47-4efa-a779-18a72d807721).
